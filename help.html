The Top of the Page

The Settings Row sets the assumptions shared across the whole plan. Directly beneath it, the Outcome Strip answers the core question — will my money last? — and the sticky Section Bar lets you jump anywhere on the page.

Elements

Settings Row
Current Age, Retirement Age, Finish Age, Filing As, Inflation, and Backtest from Year. On an existing plan, changing Retirement Age shifts the Retire phase along with any salaries ending and Social Security or pensions starting on the old retirement date (Social Security never moves before age 62). A one-line note shows what moved, and changing the age back restores it. Each scenario keeps its own settings.
Will My Money Last?
Three clickable summary tiles side by side, each labeled with its assumptions: Plan as entered (ending net worth in nominal and today’s dollars, plus whether the plan ever runs out), In 1,000 simulated markets (Monte Carlo survival rate and median), and With spending guardrails. Tiles dim briefly while recalculating after an edit; click any tile to jump to its section.
Section Bar
Stays pinned to the top of your screen as you scroll and highlights your current section. Click any label to jump straight there.
Collapsed Sections
Spreadsheet and Visualizer start collapsed to keep the page focused. Click Show in either heading to open it; your open/collapsed preference is remembered across visits.

Your Timeline

The Timeline plots your portfolio across every phase of your life on one interactive curve, marks major milestones, and controls the active month shown throughout the rest of the page. Use the scenario chip in its heading to switch between saved plans.

Elements

The Wealth Arc
Plots the active metric (Net Worth by default) across the entire simulation. Shows your starting value on the left, ending value in bold on the right, and a checkpoint at retirement, with ages along the axis and calendar years at both ends.
Phase Bands & Growth Rates
Shaded bands mark each life phase — Accumulate in green, Retire in coral — with a dashed line at retirement. Each band displays its annualized nominal and real growth rate (e.g., 7.2%/yr · 4.0% real), or a dash if the phase starts or ends at $0 or below.
Today’s Dollars
A lighter companion curve shows the arc adjusted for inflation in today’s dollars, paired with today’s $ figures under the start, end, and cursor values whenever you are viewing a balance metric.
Milestone Pins
Pins along the curve mark major asset milestones — buying or selling a home, opening or paying off a mortgage, and starting Social Security or a pension. Click any pin to edit that asset.
“You are Here” Cursor
A vertical marker displaying the selected date, your age, and value. Drag anywhere on the curve to scrub through time, or click the curve and press ← / → to step one month (hold Shift for a full year). Every chart, badge, and view updates together.
Month & Year Details
Directly beneath the timeline, two matching rows show the selected month and its full calendar year: Income received, Expenses, Taxes, and Cash flow, followed by Withdrawn, Asset growth, and Net worth — plus a Tax year strip underneath. How to read it.
Phase Chips & Edit Popover
Chips below the arc list each phase and its age range. Click a chip (or its dot on the axis) and pick Edit to adjust the life event, or click + Life event to add a new phase.
Scenario Menu
Click the scenario chip in the Timeline heading (press Escape to close) to switch between complete, independent plans. Each row shows a sparkline preview, note, and ending value, with buttons on the active row to rename (✏️) or delete (🗑️). A shared plan opened with Open is labeled not saved and stays strictly in memory until you click ＋ Save as scenario, switch plans, or choose Close without saving.
Date Controls & ⚡ AI Summary
Use the Year and Month dropdowns in the upper right to jump to an exact date, or click ▶ to play forward one month per second (scrub the arc or change a dropdown to pause). Click ⚡ in the heading for a copyable Markdown summary of your timeline and phases.

How To

Set up your life phases
1 Set your Current Age, Retirement Age, and Finish Age in the Settings Row. On a new plan, Accumulate and Retire follow these ages automatically; on a loaded plan, changing Retirement Age moves the retirement milestone (see Settings Row).
2 Click any phase chip below the arc (or its dot on the axis) and choose Edit to open the life event dialog.
3 Click ⇄ on any asset card to configure its fund transfers for the currently selected phase — so Accumulate and Retire can route money differently.
Navigate to a specific date
1 Pick any date with the Year and Month dropdowns. Every chart, badge, portfolio card, and detail view updates immediately.
2 Or drag anywhere on the wealth arc to scrub month by month — or focus the arc and press ← / → (Shift jumps a year).
3 Click ▶ next to the month selector to animate forward one month per second across sparklines, Flows bars, and the Visualizer.
Read Month & Year Details
1 Scrub the timeline cursor to any month. The Month & Year Details panel beneath the timeline stays open and always shows that month alongside its full calendar year (partial first or last years note which months they cover).
2 Both rows use identical columns so the year equals the sum of its months: Cash flow = Income received − Expenses − Taxes. Income received counts external money coming in (wages, Social Security, pensions, interest, and dividends) — not withdrawals from your own brokerage or retirement accounts. Expenses includes living expenses, home maintenance/insurance, and the full mortgage payment (principal plus interest). Click Income received, Expenses, or Taxes to open an itemized popover breakdown. Net worth is a balance rather than a sum, showing the ending figure and net change for the year.
3 Withdrawn shows the cash that actually left your accounts to cover obligations — living costs, the full mortgage payment, home carrying costs, property tax, and taxes drawn from accounts. Because it includes property tax and account-paid taxes, it is typically larger than the Expenses column. Click it for a full breakdown. While you are working, its tax line is often $0 because payroll withholding comes straight out of your paycheck rather than an account. A pink unfunded badge means obligations exceeded what eligible accounts could pay.
4 Tax year beneath the rows summarizes the year like a tax return: Federal taxes owed (FICA, ordinary income tax, capital-gains tax, and NIIT), AGI, MAGI, effective tax rates, and any ACA Premium Tax Credit. Because mid-plan annual true-ups and NIIT settle the following January (while a plan ending in December folds its final settlement into that closing December), the Taxes column reflects cash paid during the calendar year while Tax year reflects what that year owed. A trailing partial year before December is left unsettled and has no tax-year row.
5 Click Spreadsheet → or Credit memos → in the heading to jump straight to that exact month in the detail views (auto-expanding the Spreadsheet if collapsed).
Create and manage scenarios
1 Click the scenario chip in the Timeline heading and select ＋ New scenario. Give it a name and optional note, and choose whether to start blank or duplicate your current scenario (including all assets, phase transfers, settings, and guardrails).
2 Click any scenario in the menu to switch immediately. Every scenario is fully independent.
3 On the active row, click ✏️ to rename or edit its note, or 🗑️ to delete it (available whenever you have more than one scenario).
Quick Start
1 On an empty plan, the welcome banner offers four starter templates — complete with income, accounts, housing, expenses, and transfer rules for both Accumulate and Retire — or choose Start from Scratch.
2 The templates match your Filing As setting: Early Career, Mid Career, Pre-Retirement, and Retired for Single; or Young Couple, Dual Income Couple, Couple Nearing Retirement, and Retired Couple for Married Filing Jointly (with two salaries and two Social Security streams).
3 Loading a template sets its default ages and filing status (for example, Mid Career starts at 45 and retires at 67). You can adjust Retirement Age or any asset right afterward.

Your Portfolio

Your Portfolio displays every asset in the active scenario across two views: Assets (grouped cards for everything you own, earn, and spend) and Flows (a visual breakdown of where every dollar moved and which rules sent it).

Elements

⚠️ What Needs Attention
A collapsible bar above your assets reporting issues in your plan (not app bugs). Findings are grouped into 💸 Paying for the plan (unpaid obligations and accounts that ran dry and spilled over) and 🔧 Needs setting up (missing funding accounts, capped retirement contributions, unmet RMDs, or Social Security dated before 62). If the plan runs out of money, that month leads as a callout at the top.

The panel opens itself once when issues first appear; after that, the badge updates quietly so it won’t interrupt you while editing. (For reporting a bug in the app itself, use Issues 🐛 in the page header.)
⚠️ on an Asset Card
A warning badge on a card means an obligation tied to this asset went unpaid. It does not fire merely because a retirement account reached $0. Open the card’s 👓 view to see which months failed and why.
👓 Asset View
Hover any asset card and click 👓 to inspect read-only simulation results across This month, This year (year-to-date), and Life to date. Underneath the numbers, plain-English rule notes explain every rule that fired — such as why a Roth withdrawal owed no tax, where tax on untaxed income was paid, what was withheld from a pension, or why a contribution was capped.
Asset Columns
Assets are organized into six collapsible columns — Income, Capital, Real Estate, Retirement, Expenses, and Taxes — each showing its group total. Expanding or collapsing a column also controls which individual assets appear in the Micro Projection chart.
Asset Cards
Each card displays the asset’s value at the cursor date, a sparkline across the full plan with a “you are here” dot, and monthly inflow / growth / outflow arrows. Badges flag closed assets (⛔), unpaid obligations (⚠️), and one-time events (💰). Hover for quick actions — ✎ edit, ⇄ transfers, 👓 view results, ✕ remove — or click the card body to highlight it across the charts.
Assets / Flows Toggle
Switch between Assets (grouped cards) and Flows (where money moved and which transfer rules sent it). Both views follow the timeline cursor, and Flows always shows the transfer rules active in the cursor’s life phase.
Viewing Badge
Shows the selected month and active metric. Click any viewing badge on the page to cycle metrics everywhere at once.
Taxes Column
A dedicated summary column breaking down FICA, ordinary income tax, capital-gains tax, Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT), ACA Premium Tax Credit settlement, and property tax. Click any tax row to pulse the contributing assets across all columns.

Most rows annualize the selected month; NIIT and Premium Tax Credit settle once per year and show their trailing 12-month total instead (appearing only when non-zero).
🩺 ACA Health Insurance
An expense card for marketplace coverage with federal Premium Tax Credits calculated automatically. Enter the full monthly premium before credit and your household’s benchmark silver premium (from HealthCare.gov). Set Premium Growth % (defaults to plan inflation), Estimated Income (ACA MAGI for years without a prior full marketplace year), Advance Credit % (if taking less subsidy upfront), and optional Household Size. Leaving the finish date blank ends coverage automatically in December before age 65. See Rules for full credit details.
Where the Money Went
At the top of the Flows view, one bar per source of money shows where every dollar actually went during the selected month or calendar year (Month | Year toggle): into your accounts (purple), spent (red), or paid in tax (amber), tagged with destination group icons.

The summary tiles above reconcile directly: Came in − Spent − Tax paid = Into your accounts, matching Month & Year Details aside from unplaced income, unpaid bills (listed beside the bars), one-time windfalls/purchases, and the one-month property-tax escrow lag (noted right on the Tax paid tile).
Rules in Force
Lists every transfer rule active in the current phase beside what it actually moved — clarifying whether each percentage applies to gross pay (401(k)/Traditional IRA), take-home pay (Roth/brokerage/savings), or the bill (expenses), with on-close sweeps on their own rows. Inactive rules that moved $0 fold away cleanly.

Moved automatically lists engine-managed flows: payroll and distribution withholding, estimated taxes, prior-year settlements, RMDs, leftover take-home pay, backstop payouts, account spillovers, and sale proceeds. Hover any bar segment or rule to highlight its counterpart, or click a rule to edit it in the phase where it was defined.
Fund Transfer Modal
Open by clicking ⇄ on an asset card or clicking a rule in Flows. Set monthly (0.1% steps) and on-close transfer percentages independently for each life phase (such as Accumulate vs. Retire).
Funding Backstop & Automatic Failover
When no transfer covers a bill, mortgage, property tax, or tax payment — or when a funding account hits $0 mid-payment — the engine automatically steps across your everyday accounts in order: Cash → Savings → Taxable Account → US Treasury → Corporate Bond, draining across multiple accounts in a single draw if needed.

Before age 60, retirement accounts are never drawn automatically. At age 60 and older (the whole-year equivalent of 59½), once all everyday accounts are empty, failover continues into retirement accounts in tax-efficient order: Traditional IRA → 401(k) → Roth IRA (Roth strictly last resort). Leftover take-home pay, RMDs, sale proceeds, and tax refunds always deposit into your first open everyday account (even at $0) and never into retirement accounts. Anything no eligible account can cover is logged as Unfunded.
⚡ AI Summary & + Add Asset
Click ⚡ for a structured Markdown summary of your portfolio, or click + at the bottom-right to add a new asset, income stream, expense, or debt.

How To

Add an asset
1 Click the + button at the bottom-right of Your Portfolio.
2 Pick an asset type (such as Working Income, Taxable Account, Home, or ACA Health Insurance). The type sets how the asset grows, is taxed, and interacts with transfers.
3 Enter a unique display name, start/end dates, starting value, and annual return rate, then click Save. Adding a future home purchase automatically opens the Purchase Funding dialog (for outright purchase or down payment + mortgage).
Configure fund transfers
1 Click ⇄ on an asset card, or click any rule under Rules in force in the Flows view.
2 Enter a percentage (0–100%) for each target account — for example, routing 15% of salary to a 401(k) and 10% to a brokerage account during Accumulate.
3 Select the Retire phase on the timeline to configure separate retirement-phase withdrawal rules (such as drawing from IRA or brokerage first to control tax brackets).
Edit or delete an asset
1 Hover over any asset card and click ✎ to edit it, 💰 to add one-time windfalls or purchases, or ✕ to delete it.
2 In the edit dialog, update names, dates, balances, rates, or purchase funding sources and click Save.
3 Deleting an asset removes it and its own outgoing transfers from the current scenario. Any inbound transfers, life-event closes, or purchase-funding links pointing to it stay parked by name (flagged in What needs attention) and reconnect automatically if you later add an asset with the same name — or you can remove them directly in the source asset or life event.
Work through “What needs attention”
1 Start with the top callout if present — the month the plan runs out of money. That is the first month an obligation exceeded every eligible account, and later alerts usually stem from it.
2 Check 🔧 Needs setting up next. These highlight setup issues like missing everyday accounts, contributions hitting IRS caps, deferred Social Security, or unmet RMDs.
3 Use “Ran dry” notices as an early warning. Bills were still paid by the next account in your failover chain, so scrub the timeline to that month and click 👓 to see what drained the account.
4 Keep the age-60 failover boundary in mind: before age 60, retirement accounts are never drawn automatically, so an unpaid bill before 60 alongside healthy 401(k)/IRA/Roth balances means your everyday accounts ran dry early. From age 60 onward, failover automatically draws IRA → 401(k) → Roth IRA once everyday accounts empty — and you can still add explicit transfers whenever you want a custom withdrawal order.
Explore the Flows view
1 Click Flows in the Assets / Flows toggle at the top of Your Portfolio.
2 Inspect each source bar (colored by into accounts, spent, or tax) and toggle Month | Year for annual totals.
3 Hover any bar segment to highlight its matching rule under Rules in force or Moved automatically, and click any rule to edit its transfer percentages directly.

Simulations

Two built-in stress tests: Monte Carlo (1,000 market simulations drawn from 1970–2025 economic history) and Guardrails (adaptive retirement spending). Both re-run automatically after every edit, dimming briefly until fresh results arrive.

Elements

Monte Carlo Chart
Runs 1,000 seeded simulations drawn from 1970–2025 history (or from your Backtest year forward) and plots 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile bands alongside your deterministic baseline. Use the action button to pause, resume, or restart a run.
Calibrated / Historical
Calibrated (default) keeps your configured return and inflation rates and applies each historical year’s volatility around its average. Historical uses each drawn year’s raw historical returns as they happened.
Summary Cards
Under Monte Carlo: success rate (share of trials staying above $0 every month from retirement onward), median, 10th and 90th percentile endings, and plan horizon. Under Guardrails: ending portfolio value, final-year withdrawal, and total cuts and raises triggered.
With Guardrails Checkbox
Applies your active guardrail rules inside all 1,000 Monte Carlo trials so you can see how flexible spending raises survival rates and cushions bad markets.
Guardrails Chart
Plots your portfolio balance and annual withdrawals on dual axes, marking every year a preservation cut or prosperity raise fired.
Guardrail Parameters
Click the inline summary line to adjust Target Rate (default 5%), Preservation (15%), Prosperity (15%), and Adjustment (15%). Bands are relative drift around the target — at the defaults, cuts fire above 5.75% and raises fire below 4.25%. Saved per scenario, with a one-click reset to defaults.

How To

Run a Monte Carlo simulation
1 Monte Carlo runs automatically whenever your plan loads or changes. Click its button to pause, resume, or re-run.
2 Check the percentile fan chart — the 50th percentile shows a typical outcome while the 10th percentile shows a harsh market stress test.
3 Toggle With Guardrails to see how adaptive spending changes survival across all 1,000 trials.
Configure Guardrails
1 Set your Target Rate (e.g., 5%) — your baseline annual withdrawal rate from investable assets in retirement.
2 Set the Preservation and Prosperity bands. Each year after retirement, the engine compares your actual withdrawal rate (annual expenses divided by investable assets) against the target — cutting spending if it drifts too high and raising spending if it drifts low.
3 Set the Adjustment percentage — how much open Monthly Expenses scale when a guardrail triggers (ACA Health Insurance premiums are exempt since marketplace prices are fixed for the year). Pre-retirement years are never adjusted.
4 The Guardrails chart updates immediately after each change, marking every spending cut or raise on the timeline.

Projections

Two side-by-side stacked bar charts showing your plan’s trajectory across time: Macro displays grouped asset categories across the full plan, while Micro zooms into individual assets from whichever columns are expanded in Your Portfolio.

Elements

Macro Projection Chart
Stacked bars grouped by asset category over the full timeline for the active metric (time buckets automatically adapt from monthly to quarterly, semi-annual, or annual depending on plan length).
Micro Projection Chart
Shows individual assets from the columns currently expanded in Your Portfolio, with a dedicated metric dropdown tailored to those asset groups.
Metric Selector
Switch between Net Worth, Net Worth Change, Growth, Cash Flow, Income, Expenses, Taxes, and more. Changing the Macro metric updates every section on the page.
Click-to-Cycle Viewing Badges
Every Viewing: Month Year · Metric badge across the page is clickable — click one to step through the main metrics and update the timeline, cards, charts, and Visualizer together.
Phase & Cursor Markers
Vertical dashed lines mark life-event phase transitions (such as Accumulate → Retire), and a black ▼ marker tracks your current timeline date across charts.
Fullscreen View
Click ⛶ in the upper-right of either chart to expand it fullscreen; press Escape or click × to return.

How To

Analyze your portfolio trajectory
1 Use the Macro Projection chart to check the overall arc — growth during your working years and sustainable drawdown in retirement.
2 Click any viewing badge (or use the metric dropdown) to compare Net Worth, Net Worth Change (shows when withdrawals begin outpacing growth), Growth, Cash Flow, Income, Expenses, and Taxes.
3 Expand or collapse columns in Your Portfolio to focus the Micro chart on specific accounts, and scrub the timeline to inspect any date.

Details

Two complementary views into the simulation’s numbers: Credit Memos for every individual ledger entry, and Reports for monthly and annual financial statements.

Elements

Credit Memos
A transaction-by-transaction ledger for the selected month — paychecks, transfers, expenses, tax withholding, growth, and settlements — recorded live by the engine as it ran. Informational, escrow, and tax-recognition entries are badged NON-CASH so you can separate them from cash flows at a glance.
Memos Worth Watching
Unfunded — an obligation no eligible account could pay (the first one marks when the plan runs out). Spillover from depleted … — an account hit $0 mid-payment and the failover chain covered the rest. Contribution capped — a transfer hit an annual IRS cap or ran out of paycheck headroom. Social Security deferred to age 62 — a benefit dated before 62 held until 62. Each is also surfaced in plain language in What needs attention and in each asset’s 👓 view.
Reports
Monthly and annual summaries for the cursor date: income by source, deductions, contributions, debt paydown, cash flow, taxes, and growth. Annual reports also show AGI, purpose-specific MAGI lines (for NIIT, senior deduction, ACA credit, plus reference figures for IRMAA, IRA deductions, and Roth limits), and ACA Premium Tax Credit totals. Taxes show cash paid in the calendar year, with any prior-year true-up or NIIT broken out under Prior-Year Settlement.
⚡ AI Summaries
Both Credit Memos and Reports have their own ⚡ buttons to copy clean Markdown summaries of the active period.
Synced to the Timeline
Both panels scroll and update automatically whenever you move the timeline cursor.

How To

Inspect a specific month’s finances
1 Move the timeline cursor or use the date dropdowns to pick the month you want to audit.
2 Check Credit Memos to verify individual transactions, transfer splits, failover draws, and tax withholding.
3 Check Reports for monthly and annual totals — income, expenses, taxes, AGI/MAGI, and ending balances.

Spreadsheet

A full monthly data table across every asset and metric for the entire simulation. Starts collapsed; click Show in its heading to open it.

Elements

Data Grid
One row per simulated month with exact-to-the-cent columns for every active asset metric (including NIIT and ACA Premium Tax Credit columns). All-zero columns are omitted automatically to keep the table compact.
Copy & Download
Click Copy (📋) to copy the table for Excel or Google Sheets, or Download (⬇️) to save it as a CSV file. Numbers export cleanly without $ signs so formulas work immediately.

How To

Export your data
1 Click Show in the Spreadsheet heading to expand the monthly table.
2 Click Copy to place the entire table on your clipboard for Excel or Google Sheets.
3 Or click Download to save a standard .csv file to your computer.

Visualizer

A live plumbing diagram of your money flow at the cursor date: income feeds in from the left, investment and retirement accounts sit above a central pipe, expenses, housing, and taxes drain below, and a Wealth Tank on the right tracks your portfolio level. Starts collapsed; click Show in its heading to open it.

Elements

Horizontal Conduit
The central pipe connecting every stream. Its color shifts from green (net inflows) to red (net outflows). Hover over it for a tooltip showing month-over-month net worth change and total portfolio value.
Income Sources (Left)
Salary, self-employment, pensions, and Social Security feed in from the left. Line thickness uses logarithmic scaling so smaller and larger streams stay readable side by side.
Capital & Retirement (Above)
Brokerage, savings, bonds, IRAs, and 401(k)s sit above the pipe. In Cash Flow mode, each asset displays green inflow lines (contributions, deposits) and red outflow lines (withdrawals, distributions); hover any line for itemized transfer routes.
Expenses, Housing & Taxes (Below)
Living expenses, mortgages, home costs, and an aggregated Taxes drain (combining FICA, income tax, capital gains, NIIT, and property tax) flow below the pipe.
Wealth Tank (Right)
Fill height always scales to total portfolio value across the plan’s range — red below 15% of peak, blue from 15–50%, and green above 50% — while its label shows the active metric total.
Metric-Driven Modes
Follows whichever global metric is active: Cash Flow shows monthly inflow/outflow pipes, Net Worth shows each asset’s balance share (blue positive, red negative), and Growth combines appreciation, interest, and dividends. Inactive or closed assets dim with a ⛔ badge.

How To

Read your money flow
1 Scrub the timeline cursor (or click Play) — the Visualizer updates live for every month.
2 Click any viewing badge to switch metrics (Net Worth → Net Worth Change → Growth → Cash Flow…) and watch the pipes and Wealth Tank shift accordingly.
3 Hover the conduit or individual flow lines for exact dollar breakdowns, and scrub across retirement to watch the central pipe flip from green accumulation to red drawdown.

Test Bench

The Test Bench (formerly the Maximizer) stress-tests your plan without altering your saved scenario. Its Spending test finds the highest monthly spending your plan can sustain while staying funded, meeting your ending-balance target, and surviving your chosen share of simulated markets. Its Routing test searches for better income-split and expense-funding percentages to squeeze even more sustainable spending out of the same assets.

Elements

Conditions
The card at the top holds the conditions about the world that every test and the chart run under: today, Market returns. Reset to as entered puts every condition back (it is greyed out when they already are). On a phone the card shrinks to one line naming the conditions in force; tap Edit to change them.
Spending and Routing Tabs
The two tests sit on their own tabs, and each keeps its result while you look at the other. A tab shows running… or searching… while its test works. The holdings chart sits under both tabs and opens on the column that goes with the tab: Spend the most on Spending, With this routing on Routing.
Run, and What Dims
Nothing runs by itself. Change as many conditions and limits as you like, then click Run (Spending) or Search (Routing). Until you do, a result found under other settings is dimmed, with one line saying what changed (“Conditions changed. Run again.”), and it cannot be saved. Put a setting back and the result is current again. A click on the curve is the one exception: it sets the floor and runs at once.
Found Under
The first line of every result names what it was found under: each condition not at its as-entered setting (for example Optimistic (+1%)), or your plan as entered, then the data the simulated markets drew from (for example Calibrated 1970–2025). The routing list adds its floor when it is above $0, since the floor lives on the Spending tab. The chart’s caption repeats the line for the column it shows.
Spending Limits
On the Spending tab, the solver maximizes monthly spending subject to three limits: Never runs out (always on — zero unpaid bills in any month), Leaves at least (minimum ending net worth in today’s dollars, defaulting to $0), and Lasts in …% of simulated markets (on by default at 90%; uncheck to solve strictly on your fixed return assumptions).
Simulated-Markets Limit
Maximum spending on a fixed return path leaves no buffer for bad markets. With this limit enabled, the solver lowers spending until your plan stays above $0 net worth from retirement onward in your target share of 300 simulated markets (taking ~10 seconds per step, up to 10 steps), then confirms the answer across 1,000 markets. When this limit lowers the answer, a grey dot on the curve shows the fixed-path ceiling and a note reports the monthly cost of that safety margin.
Market Returns
A condition: switch between Pessimistic (−1%/yr), Moderate (as entered), and Optimistic (+1%/yr) to stress-test your stock and bond return assumptions (Brokerage, IRA, Roth IRA, 401(k), Treasuries, and Corporate Bonds — leaving inflation, wages, cash, housing, and debt untouched). Applies across the whole Test Bench, resets to Moderate on reload, and never modifies your saved plan’s return rates.
The Spending Curve
Plots monthly spending (horizontal) against ending net worth (vertical), both in today’s dollars. Every point is a real simulation run. The hollow dot is your plan as entered, the filled dot is the solved maximum, a grey dot (if shown) is the fixed-path ceiling, and the hatched region is where the plan runs out. Hover any point for details, or click anywhere on the curve to set that ending balance as your floor and re-solve.
The Result Table
Compares As entered side by side with Spend the most (and With this routing when you run a routing search): monthly and lifetime spending (excluding taxes), ending net worth, and whether the plan runs out. If your entered plan runs out, its spending is labeled paid (what it could afford, not what was billed), and the headline gives the safe spending as a percentage of your entered expenses. Plans with an ACA Health Insurance card split spending into before 65 and from 65 and total the Premium Tax Credit.
In Simulated Markets Row
Tests each table column across the exact same 300 seeded markets (without guardrails) and fills in a few seconds after the main answer. Because 300 trials is smaller than the Simulations section’s 1,000, the rate can vary by a point or two.
With Guardrails Row
Evaluates each column under your current Guardrails settings from retirement onward (showing ending wealth plus total cuts and raises), without changing your guardrail inputs.
What Stops It
A plain-English sentence naming the exact limit blocking higher spending. When an unpaid bill stops the plan before age 60, it names the month and any retirement accounts still holding money (since automatic failover protects retirement accounts before 60). Click Show that month to jump straight to that month in Month & Year Details.
Save as a Scenario
Saves the solved monthly expenses into a new scenario and switches to it, leaving your original plan untouched. Only monthly expense amounts change; even if solved under Pessimistic or Optimistic returns, the new scenario preserves your original return rates and notes the outlook used.
Routing: Search Depth
On the Routing tab, controls how thoroughly the routing test explores transfer rules: Depth 1 runs 1 climb from your current plan; Depth 2 adds 4 nearby climbs; Depth 3 adds 5 random climbs; Depth 4 adds 10 genetic searches (10,000 runs each). Runs locally in parallel across your CPU cores, with an upfront time estimate shown beside the control. Routings are compared with the Spending test’s answer, so Search runs that test too when its answer is missing or was found under another floor or other conditions; the tab says so before you click. When Leaves at least is above $0, the tab names it, since it ranks the routings.
Routing: Ranked Results
Lists discovered routings best-first by monthly spending, showing the monthly gain and how many transfer rules changed (grouping identical findings into one row). Click Stop at any time to keep results finished so far. A summary counts how many searches landed within 1% of the best; a lone winner is marked Alone at the top so you know whether a deeper search is worth trying.
Routing: Proposed Changes
Shows the selected routing’s phase-by-phase transfer changes (current % → proposed %) and adds a third column (With this routing) to the result table with its own simulated-markets and guardrails rows. Click any row in the list to compare alternatives.
Routing: What Stays Untouched
Never modifies home, mortgage, or debt rules; account-to-account transfers; pensions or Social Security into retirement accounts; or retirement withdrawals in phases starting before age 60 (locked at your entered values since the early-withdrawal penalty is not charged). Annual 401(k) and IRA caps remain enforced.
Routing: Saving
Choose Save this routing as a scenario (keeps your current spending) or Save it with its spending (updates monthly expenses to the solved maximum too). Your original scenario is never altered.
The Holdings Chart
Draws any column of the result table (As entered, Spend the most, or With this routing) as stacked bars in today’s dollars on a shared vertical scale so switching columns immediately shows what changed. Each bar reflects the end of its period, so the final bar matches Left at the end. Hover any bar for per-group totals.
Result Notes
Contextual notes appear under the table when relevant — for example, noting if your ACA health plan ends at age 65 with no post-65 health expense in the plan (since Medicare premiums aren’t added automatically), or if a non-monotonic dip caused higher spending to leave more ending wealth.

How To

Find how much your plan can spend
1 On the Spending tab, leave Leaves at least at $0 to spend down fully, or enter your desired ending balance in today’s dollars. Set any conditions in the card above first; nothing runs until you click Run.
2 Click Run. With the simulated-markets limit enabled, solving takes roughly 10 seconds to 2 minutes (or a few seconds with it off).
3 Read the headline for your safe monthly spending and What stops it to see which limit bound first.
4 Click anywhere along the curve to explore the trade-off between spending more today and leaving more at the end (cached survival rates make follow-up runs quick).
Search for a better routing
1 Open the Routing tab, pick a Depth (start with the default; go deeper if multiple searches don’t agree) and review the estimated time beside it.
2 Click Search — results stream into the ranked list as searches finish.
3 Review the top routing’s changes and its In simulated markets row. Because routing searches optimize on your fixed return path, set Leaves at least above $0 if you want extra market cushion.
4 Save the winning routing as a new scenario (with or without its solved spending).
Keep a result
1 Click Save as a scenario and enter a name.
2 The app switches to the new scenario automatically; your original scenario remains available in the scenario menu.
3 Whenever you edit your plan, a condition or a limit later, any Test Bench output it affects dims to show it is out of date — click Run or Search again to refresh.
